## Overview

Detecting pests in field photography is object detection under unhelpful conditions: small targets, cluttered backgrounds, and classes that look alike. This project worked the **AgroPest-12** benchmark — **11,502 training, 1,095 validation and 546 test images across 12 classes** — and compared three approaches: RT-DETR, YOLOv8m, and a YOLO + SVM hybrid.

## The RT-DETR pipeline

The transformer detector needed a data path that did not exist off the shelf. YOLO-format annotations were converted to COCO, then fed through a **custom PyTorch wrapper** so Hugging Face's `AutoImageProcessor` and the Supervision toolkit could drive fine-tuning on a custom dataset.

A pretrained **RT-DETR R50** was fine-tuned across several configurations. The best short run reached **0.77 mAP@50** and **0.49 mAP@50:95** — competitive coarse localisation, weaker under strict IoU.

## Where it goes wrong

The confusion matrix shows that miss is part of a pattern. **Beetles became a catch-all class.** 63 objects from other classes were predicted as beetles, including **28 of 47 wasps** and **18 of 44 bees**. Wasp recall falls to 9% and bee recall to 27%, while moths, snails and weevils all stay above 90%.

The other big loss is **objects that were never detected at all**: 160 of 689 labelled objects, concentrated in caterpillars (31), ants (29) and grasshoppers (25). Those are the small, clustered and well-camouflaged targets. Caterpillars and earthworms also get mixed up with each other, which makes sense for two long, segmented bodies.

## The more useful result is the ceiling

Runs at 2, 5, 7 and 15 epochs were compared, along with batch size, early stopping, and augmentation. The model **plateaued and then overfit** rather than continuing to improve, and augmentation barely moved it: roughly **0.48 mAP@50:95 augmented against a 0.47–0.49 baseline**.

That is a negative result, and it is the one worth reporting. The constraint was data and compute, not hyperparameters — so more tuning would have been effort spent in the wrong place.

## Comparison across the team's approaches

<Note>
  The two results below are **team results**, included for comparison. They are not claimed as sole implementations; the RT-DETR pipeline and analysis above are the personal contribution.
</Note>

- **YOLOv8m** reached 91.7% precision, 85.1% recall and 91.9% mAP@0.5 at 9.9 ms per image — the strongest detector in the comparison.
- **YOLO + SVM** combined 200-dimensional HSV, HOG, Hu-moment, Laplacian and statistical features with an RBF SVM. It achieved **93.1% mean five-fold cross-validation accuracy against roughly 40% on held-out test data** — a textbook cross-validation-to-test collapse, traced to the feature pipeline and SMOTE resampling generalising poorly.
