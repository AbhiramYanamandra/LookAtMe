## Overview

A custom-built nine-key macropad: a 3×3 grid of mechanical switches with per-key RGB lighting and programmable layers, so each key can do different things depending on the active layer.

## What the model shows

The model at the top of this page is built from the board's own **KiCad STEP export**, with the XIAO and part colours the export drops added back, inside the enclosure described below. It is a CAD model, not a photograph of a finished unit. Take it apart to see each layer, from the switches down to the bottom tray, and hover any part to name it.

## The board

The PCB is designed in **KiCad** around a **Seeed XIAO RP2040**, carrying nine mechanical switches and nine diodes in a 3×3 matrix, plus an **SSD1306 OLED** on I²C and a rotary encoder.

It was taken all the way through to a manufacturable package — **Gerbers, drill files, bill of materials, position data and production ZIPs** — rather than stopping at a schematic that works in simulation.

## Firmware

Firmware runs **CircuitPython with KMK**, presenting the board as a USB HID device over a nine-key matrix. The rotary encoder is mapped to volume down and up, with push to mute.

## Enclosure

The finished PCB was exported to **STEP** and the enclosure modelled around it in **Fusion 360**, so the electrical and mechanical designs are dimensioned against the same source geometry rather than measured by hand.

The case has 2 mm walls and a top plate 5 mm above the board, the standard MX plate height, with a pocket for each switch, a window for the OLED and header, M2 standoffs at the four mounting holes, and a slot for the XIAO's USB-C port. The 3D model shows the board inside it: switch the case to clear, or hide it, to see the PCB.

<Note>
  An earlier prototype of this board used an ESP32-S3; the final design is the XIAO RP2040 described above. Per-key RGB appears in the summary but is not covered by the build record this page draws on.
</Note>
