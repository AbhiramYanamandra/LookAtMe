## Overview

A clap detector is a trivial algorithm sitting behind a genuinely awkward data path. On a Kria KV260, audio has to get from an **I²S microphone**, through FPGA fabric, across the programmable-logic / processing-system boundary, and into software — in real time, without dropping samples.

The final system streams microphone data through a **FIFO and AXI4-Stream into AXI DMA**, landing it in memory where **ARM-side PYNQ/Python** performs threshold detection and drives LED and Bluetooth output.

<Note>
  Detection happens on the ARM side in PYNQ, not in FPGA DSP. The programmable logic handles capture and streaming; the decision logic is software.
</Note>

## Where the work actually went

Almost none of it was the detection.

**Debugging the capture path** was the bulk of the project — the I²S interface, the FIFO, and the PS–PL boundary where timing and handshaking problems surface as silence or garbage rather than as errors. Stabilising that path is what made everything downstream possible.

**Bringing up the software side** meant standing up Ubuntu and PYNQ on the board and establishing the **first end-to-end clap-to-LED path** — the moment that proves the whole chain, hardware through software, actually carries data.

The system was demonstrated over **100+ iterations** in the lab.

<Note>
  A team project. The audio pipeline debugging is a contribution rather than sole ownership; the Ubuntu/PYNQ bring-up and the first working end-to-end path are supported as personal contributions.
</Note>
