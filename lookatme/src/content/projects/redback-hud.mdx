## Overview

A Formula SAE car needs a display behind the steering wheel. It shows the driver lap time, battery state and temperatures at a glance, and it says clearly when something is wrong. On **UNSW Redback Racing** I worked on the firmware for that display: an **STM32 U-series** microcontroller running **C++ on FreeRTOS**, reading vehicle data off the car's **CAN** bus and drawing it with **TouchGFX**.

The display sits at the end of a long chain. Each sensor and controller on the car publishes on CAN. The display has to pick out the values the driver needs, keep up with them, and redraw fast enough to be useful at speed, without the interface stalling while it does.

The screen at the top of this page is where the design ended up. The rest of the page is how it got there: the screen the car raced with, what it looked like on the car, and what the team changed so a driver could read it at speed.

This is the screen the car ran: a dense grid built for engineers as much as drivers. Every control value appears twice, **requested** and **actual**, so a mismatch between what was asked for and what the car did is visible immediately. Next to them sit the state of the car's other computers: whether service is **active**, whether the **Jetson** is ready, and the **SBG** GNSS solution status, accuracy and position.

That's exactly what you want while debugging on a bench. At speed, a driver needs a handful of those numbers, and needs to find them without reading.

## Where the design went next

With the team I worked on a redesign in **Figma** that goes further: keep what a driver needs and arrange everything around it. The redesign is the screen at the top of this page. It wasn't filmed running on the car.

The redesign changes four things:

- **The timer is the centre of the screen.** Lap time and the delta to the best lap are what a driver looks for. Everything else is arranged around them.
- **State has a shape and a colour.** Ready, Failed and Inactive each have their own icon treatment, and the accumulator and radiator fans, which are PDM channels, show plainly whether they're on.
- **The car's other computers report in.** A status row shows the CAN Forwarder, the Jetson and the router, beside the discharge current limit and motor and coolant temperatures.
- **Driving context is on the screen.** Brake bias, the RTD (ready-to-drive) state and the lap count sit under the timer.

## States that matter

A warning only works if the driver can't miss it. Each limit raises its own full-width red banner over the screen, and when several trip at once they stack rather than replace each other. Shutting down swaps every warning for a single blue instruction, the one thing the driver needs to do next. The display at the top plays through each of them; toggle any of them yourself to see it on its own.
