## Software that runs during a session

Redback's telemetry platform is used by race engineers while the car is running, which makes it production software with an unusually unforgiving deadline: if it is wrong or slow during a test session, the session is what gets wasted.

I delivered features across the stack — **React, Next.js, Node and TypeScript**, frontend and backend — for live vehicle monitoring and the setup decisions engineers make from it.

## Making the data comparable

Live monitoring answers "what is the car doing now." The harder question is "what did it do differently last time," and that needs the data to outlive the session.

I designed a **centralised logging pipeline** converting raw vehicle sensor output into structured records stored in **AWS S3**, so runs become traceable and configurations can be compared against each other rather than against memory. Services were containerised with **Docker** and deployed on AWS to keep live and historical data reliably reachable.

## Testing a tool people depend on

I introduced **Playwright end-to-end testing** across the telemetry workflows, **increasing automated test coverage by 20%**. On a volunteer team with rotating membership, the tests are also documentation — they state what the platform is supposed to do after the person who wrote the feature has graduated.

## The other end: firmware on the car

The same work ran down to the vehicle itself. I developed and debugged **real-time firmware for an STM32 U-series driver display HUD**, using **C++ with FreeRTOS**, reading vehicle data off **CAN** and rendering it with **TouchGFX** — live performance information in front of the driver, at a refresh rate that is useful at speed.

I also designed the **compact waterproof enclosure** for that display, with thermal management considered as part of the design. A HUD on a race car is an electronics problem and a mechanical one at the same time, and the two constraints resolve together or not at all.

## How the team worked

Agile sprints, code reviews and written documentation through Jira, Confluence and Bitbucket, across firmware, data and race-engineering sub-teams — the coordination that makes a volunteer team of this size deliver anything at all.
